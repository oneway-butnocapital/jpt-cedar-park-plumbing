Garbage Disposal Repair in Cedar Park, TX

We repair humming, jammed, leaking, and clogged garbage disposals in Cedar Park.

Call (512) 818-1571 Ask a question

Older homes around Cedar Park, Anderson Mill, Brushy Creek, and Jollyville often have a disposal that has worked through years of food scraps, detergent, and hard-water residue. The grinding chamber, splash guard, mounting ring, and drain connection all wear differently, so a sink that once cleared quickly can begin holding water or making a low hum. We check the unit beneath the kitchen sink rather than assuming a slow bowl points to one failed part.

A disposal commonly lasts about 8 to 15 years, though a utensil in the chamber, swollen food waste, or a loose wire can stop a newer unit as well. We look at the reset button, breaker, switch, sink flange, and discharge tube to trace the fault. A humming motor, backed-up sink, or water under the cabinet should not wait, because a stalled motor can overheat and standing water can damage the cabinet floor and drain connections.

Humming motor and sink odor

We hear several distinct descriptions on disposal calls: “it hums but will not spin,” “the sink fills when the dishwasher drains,” “water is leaking under the cabinet,” and “the kitchen sink smells rotten.” A hum often means the flywheel cannot turn, while a silent unit can point to the reset button, wall switch, breaker, or wiring connection. Water around the disposal can come from the sink flange, dishwasher inlet, discharge elbow, mounting assembly, or the disposal housing itself. A sour odor may come from food caught beneath the splash guard or residue sitting in the grinding chamber. These symptoms can sound alike from the kitchen counter, but each one leads to a different repair path and different parts once we inspect the motor and drain connection.

Under-sink callout testing

We start the callout by checking the cabinet for moisture, running the faucet, and testing the wall switch, breaker, and reset button. With power isolated, we inspect the grinding chamber for glass, bone fragments, cutlery, and packed food, then turn the flywheel with the proper disposal wrench where the unit allows it. We also run water through the sink and dishwasher connection, watching the discharge tube and trap for a backup or leak. A simple obstruction, reset, loose connection, splash guard, or drain fitting can often be handled during a 30- to 60-minute visit. A cracked housing, failed motor, seized mounting ring, or replacement unit that needs a specific model may require a return visit after the correct part or disposal arrives.

Mounting ring and shutoff valve

The disposal’s age affects the repair because older motors, sink flanges, and mounting assemblies may no longer match current replacement parts. Cabinet access also changes the work: a deep sink, tight corner cabinet, fixed shelving, or crowded water lines can limit room for the wrench, bucket, and drain tools. A seized shutoff valve or brittle supply line near the cabinet can add work if it must be moved or replaced to reach the disposal connection safely. We also check whether the existing electrical cord, dishwasher inlet, and drain outlet line up with a replacement model. A standard-size unit with open access and an available part usually involves less work than a cracked unit, corroded fittings, or a model that must be ordered.

Model plate and sink cabinet

Before calling, we ask customers to look for the model number on the disposal’s label or model plate, usually on the side or bottom of the unit beneath the sink. The make and model help us identify the mounting style, horsepower class, reset location, and compatible parts. Let us know which kitchen sink bowl has the disposal, whether the dishwasher connects to it, and whether the unit hums, leaks, or trips the breaker when switched on. A clear photo of the cabinet, drain pipe, and mounting ring can also show whether shelves, cleaning products, or water lines restrict access. Please tell us if water is standing in the sink or collecting on the cabinet floor when you call JPT Cedar Park Plumbing at (512) 818-1571. A leak at the disposal or discharge tube can keep soaking the cabinet base and nearby wood if the repair waits.
