Water Heater Repair in Cedar Park, TX

We repair water heater leaks, no-hot-water calls, and temperature faults.

Call (512) 818-1571 Ask a question

A wet patch outside the utility closet and a shower that never warms up both get called water heater trouble, but they point to different work. Water reaching the hallway can come from a tank, temperature-and-pressure relief valve, drain valve, or pipe connection. Cold water at the bathtub can trace back to a burner, heating element, thermostat, gas supply, or electrical circuit.

We repair both the water heater that has stopped heating and the heater that has started releasing water. We check the tank, fittings, supply lines, controls, and nearby floor before deciding whether a repair part can correct the fault. We also serve water heater calls in Cedar Park, Anderson Mill, Brushy Creek, and Jollyville.

A dry tank that simply produces no hot water may allow time for a planned repair, but water collecting around the base or running from a valve should not wait. Continued leakage can damage drywall, flooring, insulation, and the water heater cabinet, while pressure or burner faults can worsen with repeated use.

No hot water or leaking tank

Customers often call about no hot water, lukewarm water that runs out quickly, or a water heater that makes a popping or rumbling sound. An electric heater may have one failed element and still deliver a short burst of warm water, while a gas heater with an ignition or burner problem may deliver none at all.

Water under the tank can come from the temperature-and-pressure relief valve, the drain valve, a union above the heater, or corrosion at the tank seam. A dripping relief valve and a leaking tank can look alike from the floor, even though the repair work differs.

Rust-colored water, a sulfur-like odor at the hot tap, or a pilot light that will not remain lit can also lead back to the heater. Each symptom can involve a separate part and a separate amount of work, so we inspect the source before tying the call to one repair.

Water heater inspection

We start the visit by checking the rating plate, tank exterior, supply connections, shutoff valve, drain valve, and relief discharge pipe. On an electric unit, we check the disconnect, voltage at the terminals, thermostat response, and heating elements. On a gas unit, we inspect the burner compartment, ignition components, vent connection, and flame operation.

Initial testing commonly takes 30 to 60 minutes, depending on access and whether the tank must heat through a cycle. We measure water temperature, check inlet water pressure where needed, and look for moisture around threaded fittings and the tank base. A temperature-and-pressure relief valve normally needs a discharge pipe that terminates close to the floor rather than spraying into the room.

Thermostats, elements, relief valves, drain valves, and accessible connections can often be repaired during the same visit when the correct part is available. A corroded tank, seized shutoff, inaccessible piping, or a part that must be ordered may require a return visit or a separate replacement discussion.

Tank age and shutoff valve

The tank age changes the repair decision because corrosion at the seam or base cannot be corrected with a new valve or heating element. We use the serial number and visible condition of the jacket, fittings, and drain area to separate a serviceable component fault from a tank that has reached the end of its useful condition.

Access also affects the work. A heater in an open garage allows a different repair path than a unit in a tight closet, attic platform, or finished utility room with drywall, shelves, and a narrow drain pan around it.

A shutoff valve that will not turn, a corroded supply connector, or a relief valve discharge line that needs correction can add work beyond the original failed part. Special-order controls, uncommon burner parts, and any required sign-off can also move the final scope because they change the materials, return visit, or installation steps.

Model number and tank location

Before calling, find the model and serial number on the rating plate attached to the water heater jacket. Tell us whether the unit sits in a garage, closet, attic, utility room, or another space, and whether a door, stairs, or stored items restrict access.

Note whether water is running from the relief pipe, drain valve, top connections, or the bottom of the tank. If the heater still produces some hot water, mention whether the water turns cold after a few minutes or stays cold at every faucet.

For a gas unit, let us know whether the pilot or burner is lit; for an electric unit, note any tripped breaker or reset button. Call JPT Cedar Park Plumbing at (512) 818-1571 before an active tank leak spreads into the floor, wall, or ceiling below.
